Reading Comprehension Practice

Understand the passage before answering. Reread, learn useful words, and find proof in the text.

What Comprehension Means

Comprehension means the reader understands what the passage says. It is more than saying words correctly.

A child may know how to pronounce a word but still need help with meaning, order, main idea, and proof.

Main Idea

What is the passage mostly about?

Details

What facts, times, people, places, or actions matter?

Proof

Which words in the passage prove the answer?

A Simple Comprehension Routine

  1. Read the title and ask what the passage may be about.
  2. Read the passage slowly one time.
  3. Circle or say two useful words.
  4. Answer one question from the passage.
  5. Look back and point to the sentence that proves the answer.
  6. Reread once to understand more.

Practice Passage

Maria's Appointment

Maria has a doctor's appointment on Monday at 9:00 AM. She takes the bus at 8:30 AM. Her mom asks her to bring a notebook and a pencil. At the doctor's office, Maria reads a book in the waiting room. The doctor says Maria is healthy and growing well.

Questions

  1. What day is Maria's appointment?
  2. What time does Maria take the bus?
  3. What should Maria bring?
  4. What does Maria do in the waiting room?
  5. What does the doctor say about Maria?

Proof step: Find the sentence that proves each answer.

Practice Passage 2

The Garden

Tom and his grandmother plant a garden in the backyard. They plant tomatoes, carrots, and green beans. Tom waters the garden every morning. After three weeks, small green leaves appear. Tom's grandmother says, "Be patient. Good things take time." By summer, they pick fresh vegetables and make a salad together.

Questions

  1. What do Tom and his grandmother plant?
  2. Where do they plant the garden?
  3. What does Tom do every morning?
  4. What happens after three weeks?
  5. What does Tom's grandmother say?
  6. What do they make by summer?

Proof step: Find the sentence that proves each answer.

Helpful Question Types

  • Who? — Find the person or people in the passage.
  • When? — Look for days, dates, times, and order words.
  • What happened? — Find the action or event in the passage.
  • Why? — Look for the reason something happened.
  • Where? — Find the place in the passage.
  • How? — Find the way something was done.

Words That Help Young Readers

  • before — earlier than something else
  • after — later than something else
  • because — gives a reason
  • appointment — a planned visit or meeting
  • patient — willing to wait without getting upset
  • appear — to show up or become visible

For Parents and Tutors

  • Let the child use Read Aloud, whisper reading, or rereading.
  • Do not correct every small pronunciation mistake during comprehension practice.
  • Ask one question at a time.
  • Celebrate proof-finding. It builds real reading strength.
  • If the child is stuck, reread the passage together before answering.
