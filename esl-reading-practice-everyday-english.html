Everyday Reading Practice

Short passages about daily life. Read aloud support, useful words, and simple questions — all free.

Reading About Everyday Life

Children read best when the passage connects to something they know. A story about going to the store, riding the bus, or making breakfast helps a child see reading as part of real life.

Each passage is short. Each passage has a few useful words. Each passage ends with simple questions that send the child back to the text for proof.

Practice Passage

Sam's Morning

Sam wakes up at 7:00 in the morning. He eats cereal for breakfast. His mom drives him to school. Sam likes to draw pictures in his notebook. At school, Sam shows his drawing to his teacher. She puts it on the wall.

Questions

  1. What time does Sam wake up?
  2. What does Sam eat for breakfast?
  3. How does Sam get to school?
  4. What does Sam like to do in his notebook?
  5. Where does the teacher put Sam's drawing?

Proof step: Find the sentence that proves each answer.

Practice Passage 2

The Bake Sale

Lily and her dad bake cookies for the school bake sale. They make chocolate chip and oatmeal cookies. Lily puts them in a box. At school, children buy the cookies. Lily's class earns twenty dollars. They give the money to the library for new books.

Questions

  1. What do Lily and her dad bake?
  2. What two kinds of cookies do they make?
  3. Where does Lily put the cookies?
  4. How much money does the class earn?
  5. What do they do with the money?

Proof step: Find the sentence that proves each answer.

Everyday Topics for Reading

  • Morning routines and getting ready
  • Going to school and the school day
  • Family meals and cooking
  • Playing outside and with friends
  • Going to the store and running errands
  • Bedtime and evening routines
  • Helping around the house
  • Visiting family and friends

Helpful Question Types

  • Who? — Find the person or people in the passage.
  • When? — Look for days, dates, times, and order words.
  • What happened? — Find the action or event.
  • Why? — Look for the reason something happened.
  • Where? — Find the place in the passage.

Words That Help Young Readers

  • before — earlier than something else
  • after — later than something else
  • because — gives a reason
  • between — in the middle of two things
  • morning — the early part of the day
  • together — with someone else

For Parents and Tutors

  • Let the child use Read Aloud, whisper reading, or rereading.
  • Do not correct every small mistake during practice.
  • Ask one question at a time.
  • Celebrate when the child finds proof in the text — that builds real reading strength.
  • Let the child pick the passage — choice builds motivation.
